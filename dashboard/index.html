PROTOCOL INTELLIGENCE / 04 — LAUNCH CLOCK

RADAR-4 — Momentum-weighted
launch clock

Every protocol below is ranked by implied value: what its token would be worth if the record stayed as it is. Radar-2 values tenure and size. The launch clock adds a second lens — category momentum and youth — and re-weights the ranking by it. The two lenses disagree on purpose: a big, old protocol can be valuable while a small, young, fast-growing one is closer to launching.

Implied value here is implied3 = implied2 × categoryMult. The clock scores every protocol 0–100 and reads off a launch window: under 90 days, 90–180 days, or over 180 days. Nothing is hardcoded by name — every figure is computed from the snapshot's own TVL, seven-day growth, age and category.

SCORING MODEL

How the radar ranks

MODEL CLAIM · NOT A PROMISE

implied2 = max(1, round(tvl_usd × 0.30 × growthMult × tenureMult × breadthMult))

categoryMult = 1 + clamp(mean 7d of the category's board protocols, −20, +20) / 100 × 0.5

launchScore = round(0.6 × ageScore + 0.4 × growthScore)  ·  implied3 = round(implied2 × categoryMult)

age_days is whole days from listed_at to 2026-11-14, or 365 when null. growth is change_7d or 0, clamped to −75…+150. ageScore = 100 × (1 − min(age_days, 1095) / 1095): a protocol tracked for three years scores 0, a brand-new one scores near 100. growthScore = (clamped growth + 75) / 225 × 100: −75% scores 0, +150% scores 100. estDays = 30 + round(330 × (100 − launchScore) / 100): the countdown the score implies, 30 to 360 days. The window follows it: under 90 days is “soon”, 90–180 days is “mid”, over 180 days is “late”.

Why two lenses? Tenure rewards survival: a protocol tracked for two years has earned its implied value. The clock rewards the opposite — youth and momentum — because a token launch is a beginning, and beginnings belong to protocols that are young and moving. When the two disagree, the board shows both: implied3 is the ranking, launch score and window are the clock's read on the same row. A reader who wants size follows implied3; a reader who wants the next launch follows the clock.

What changed from radar-2. Round 3 ranked by implied2 alone: TVL, growth, tenure and breadth. Round 4 keeps that value and multiplies it by categoryMult, so a protocol's rank now moves with its whole category's week. A Risk Curators protocol keeps 98.8 cents of its implied2 on the dollar this round; a CDP protocol keeps $1.05. The launch score and window are new columns, and the spotlight is now the ten highest launch scores instead of the five highest implied values. The board universe is unchanged — the same 178 rows — so any rank move between rounds is the clock's doing, not a different universe.

Exclusions: token recorded; version suffix; Bridge, Canonical Bridge, Liquid Staking, Liquid Restaking, Restaked BTC or CEX category; wrapped/staked/staking names; names beginning with a listed exchange.

Above the floor, the watchlist is the universe; below the floor, the same test applies. parentProtocol is absent from the snapshot, so the watchlist array is the authority above the floor. The data records no token; it never proves that none exists. One protocol in this snapshot grew +4,448% in a week — the category clamp exists so that single week cannot lift its whole category. Twenty-two rows the starter carried are not here: versioned pools, exchanges, and wrapped or staked products are not launches of their own.

“A board that ranks nothing by value is not a ranking.” Every implied value on this board is different, and rank 1 is the largest. If rounding ever ties two rows, the lower one is shaved by $1 and the detail view says so.

READER'S GUIDE

How to read this board

  1. Start with the heatmap. A green category is moving as a group; its multiplier lifts every member's implied value. A red category is the opposite. The heatmap is the clock's weather report. Click any cell to filter the board to that category.
  2. Pick a lens. Sort by implied value for size, or by launch score for the next launch. The top-ten spotlight is the clock's shortlist; the board's top rows are the value shortlist.
  3. Open a row. The detail view recomputes every number from the row's own figures — radar-2 factors, then the launch clock — so you can check the ranking by hand. Then follow its seven farm steps.
  4. Remember what the numbers are not. Implied value is a model claim, not a price and not a promise; the launch window is a countdown implied by age and growth, not a date anyone announced. The snapshot is 2026-11-14 and does not move.
DATA & METHOD

Where every number comes from

The snapshot is the house's frozen fetch of the DefiLlama protocol universe, taken 2026-11-14. It holds 758 protocols at or above $5,000,000 of TVL, each with exactly ten fields: slug, name, category (null when DefiLlama names none), chains, tvl_usd (whole dollars, truncated), change_7d (the TVL's change over seven days in per cent, null when unreported), mcap_usd (null when it records no token), listed_at (the UTC day DefiLlama began tracking it, or null), gecko_id and symbol (both null when it records no token; DefiLlama's “-” placeholder is written as null). The watchlist names 40 of them: TVL at or above the $50,000,000 floor, no token recorded, and not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a protocol whose token already trades.

The board adds the 138 pre-token protocols below the floor that pass the same exclusion test — no token recorded, no version suffix, no Bridge / Canonical Bridge / Liquid Staking / Liquid Restaking / Restaked BTC / CEX category, no whole word wrapped, staked or staking in the name, and no name beginning with a listed exchange (Binance, Coinbase, Bybit, Kraken, Bitget, OKX, Bitfinex, Gemini). Above the floor, only the watchlist counts: the snapshot carries no parentProtocol field, so there is no way to tell a legitimate omission from a wrapped product, and the list is the authority. That is why 22 rows the starter carried are not here.

The live-token set is separate and never ranked as a launch: the 24 protocols with the largest recorded market capitalisation, excluding CEX. A protocol there already trades; its presence on this page is context, not a recommendation.
